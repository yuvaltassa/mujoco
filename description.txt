================================================================================
Fold cone contributions into the Hessian when many contacts slide
================================================================================

In the elliptic Newton solver, HessianCone rebuilds Lcone every iteration by
copying L and applying dim sparse rank-1 updates per cone-state contact. With
many sliding contacts the updates dwarf the factorization they avoid: on a
collapsing house of cards, mju_cholUpdateSparse is 63% of step time. Since a
cone contact's contribution Jc'*Hc*Jc equals (Lc'*Jc)'(Lc'*Jc), replacing its
Jacobian rows with Lc'*Jc at unit weight lets the existing J'DJ + factorize
pipeline produce the cone-inclusive Hessian in one factorization, with
unchanged sparsity.

Choose between the paths with a flop-count model computed from the fixed
sparsity patterns: rank-1 updates cost the reverse-etree path of each
contact's last dof (exactly the update closure), folding costs the
factorization plus J'DJ flops. Instrumented timings of both paths (~300k
calls, 10 scenes) show the crossover moving from ~12 to ~175 cone rows with
the factor's fill-in, and the fitted gate 10U > 3(Lflops + 2S) lands within
0.8% of a per-call oracle that always picks the faster path. The path costs
and factorization flops are computed once per solve alongside the symbolic
factorization whose pattern they describe; the remaining O(nefc) decision is
cached until a constraint changes state.

End-to-end, every tested scene is at 0.99x or better vs never folding while
keeping the folding wins (1.4-2.1x mean, 2-4x worst step). The added test
drives a sliding clump with condim 3, 4 and 6 through the folding regime and
checks sparse (folding) against dense (rank-1) accelerations; it passes under
mjUSESINGLE. cone_fold.md records the design and measurement campaign.

Based on an original implementation by Kevin Zakka.
